# Fernwood Audio — Brand Voice and Audience

**Document type:** Brand voice guide
**Last reviewed:** 2026-04-18

## Who we are talking to

Two groups, and they overlap less than you would expect.

**Working location recordists.** Documentary sound, news, wildlife. They own several
recorders already and will replace one only if it solves a specific field problem. They care
about battery figures, weight, and whether a device survives being dropped.

**Serious hobbyists.** Podcasters recording outside a studio, musicians capturing rehearsals,
people who record birdsong. They are price-sensitive and intimidated by gain staging.

## Voice

Warm, plain, and unhurried. We write like someone who has carried gear up a hill in the rain.

We describe the situation before the feature. "You will not get a second take of that bird" is
better than "32-bit float eliminates clipping". Lead with the moment, then the mechanism.

We are honest about limits, because our audience will find them anyway and will trust us more
for saying them first.

## Rules

- Concrete nouns and real situations over adjectives.
- Never quote the 7.5-hour battery figure without noting it is phantom-power-off.
- Never describe the FR-2 as waterproof. It is weather-resistant with no IP rating.
- Never imply Fernwood Studio is a DAW or can replace one.
- No exclamation marks. No "unleash", "elevate", "game-changer", "pro-grade", "studio-quality".
- Avoid gear-snobbery. Hobbyists read this too.
